# Philadelphia window: architecture and animation direction

## Current build constraint

Generated PECO proportions are approximate. **The implementation will retain PECO’s current size, position, and crown animation.** The images are visual concepts, not existing app screenshots or instructions to resize or reposition PECO.

The approved direction is a more detailed PECO body, an animated Cira facade, uneven window lights, windy trees, and falling leaves that settle and collect on the ground. Concept stills illustrate appearance; they do not demonstrate functioning animation.

These twelve stills are visual proposals for the exterior only. They are not screenshots of an implemented replacement, functioning 3D scenes, or survey-verified views from a particular address. These generated stills do not establish the implementation status of the app. PECO's existing animated crown is a requirement for the eventual build.

## What makes the skyline recognizably Philadelphia

**Make the Comcast buildings unmistakably different.** Comcast Center is a faceted, silvery-glass obelisk, with clearer corners and crown. Comcast Technology Center is taller, vertically stepped and marked by an illuminated glass blade. They should differ in height, width, top silhouette and facade rhythm; neither should become one of two interchangeable rectangular slabs. [RAMSA: Comcast Center](https://www.ramsa.com/expertise/project/comcast-center), [Foster + Partners: Comcast Technology Center](https://www.fosterandpartners.com/projects/comcast-technology-center/)

**Rebuild the Liberty crowns from their actual geometry.** One Liberty's steel-and-blue-glass architecture ends in gabled angular setbacks and a slender spire. Model the folded roof planes, setbacks and recessed corners so sunlight and night lighting describe their shape. Study Two Liberty separately against the owner's photographs rather than duplicating the first tower. The two crowns must remain individually recognizable. [One Liberty owner brochure](https://onelibertyplace.com/pdf/ONE_Liberty_Place_Brochure_2019_10.pdf), [Two Liberty owner gallery](https://2libertyplace.com/)

**Preserve PECO and refine its body.** Its shiny black glass panels call for dark reflective glazing, fine joints and a visibly narrower side face. Give its crown a convincing wraparound return, retaining the current scrolling text and timing. PECO documents an upgraded display with richer color, resolution and animation; this supports a carefully luminous crown, not a generic rooftop billboard. [Local facade reporting](https://www.inquirer.com/philly/business/20070112_inq_region_PPECO.html), [PECO's crown announcement](https://www.pecoconnection.com/2020/11/10/powering-on-pecos-iconic-crown-lights/)

**Use Cira's real faceted form.** Its sloping, cut corners change the silhouette with viewpoint. Enclos describes LED fixtures integrated into the glass facade; this provides a credible second animated landmark, with restrained color sequences inspired by the building rather than invented scrolling text on every tower. [Enclos: Cira Centre](https://enclos.com/project/cira-centre/)

**Give the foreground actual Philadelphia masonry.** The 2300 Delancey block offers stepped rooflines, brownstone and Pennsylvania blue marble, slate mansards, dormers and projecting bays. St. James adds dark brick and pale stone arches; the Neff corner house offers distinctly shaped projecting bays. Translate these into recessed windows, stone reveals, separate sills, cornice shadows and visible side walls. Vary adjoining houses without losing their continuous street rhythm. [Preservation Alliance illustrated guide](https://preservationalliance.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/RW.pdf)

Smaller details can carry considerable character: Delancey's leaded glass, ornamental iron fences, tiny gardens and carved entrances. They should be concentrated where the window view can actually reveal them. [Independence Hall Association: Delancey Place](https://www.ushistory.org/districts/rittenhouse/delan.htm)

## How to make the selected view feel premium in motion

Build genuine three-dimensional foreground architecture, branches, roof edges and street surfaces. Landmarks need real volumes and layered facades, with separate front and side lighting. Fine textures can supply masonry grain and glass variation; they cannot substitute for projecting bays, roof folds or deep window openings. Place buildings at different distances, with appropriate overlap and softer atmosphere farther away.

Retain PECO's existing scrolling surface mapping when replacing its supporting building detail. Test the message across the front and side faces, including pause and reduced-motion behavior. Crown brightness should remain legible without washing out its lettering or the nearby facade.

Use a fixed, bounded leaf population with visible falling, landing and settling phases. Share gusts across trees and falling leaves, but allow different branches to respond at different speeds. Hide recycling naturally behind foliage or accumulated leaves. Keep office-light changes slow and uneven; reserve deliberate architectural light animation primarily for PECO and the Cira-inspired treatment. Cloud reflections should move coherently across each building's differently oriented glass faces.

No car models are proposed in these views. Traffic should only return if it can meet the same visual standard as the architecture. The exterior can feel alive through light, weather, water and foliage alone.

Before implementation is accepted, compare the selected skyline against reference photographs; inspect foreground depth from the actual app camera; check daylight and night lettering, motion pause, reduced motion, mobile framing and performance while typing. The stills establish a direction, not proof that those runtime checks have already passed.
