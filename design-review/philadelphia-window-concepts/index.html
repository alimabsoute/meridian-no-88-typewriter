Architecture & motion

Real references, considered movement.

The identity comes from unequal silhouettes: Comcast Technology Center’s stepped mass and illuminated glass blade; Comcast Center’s faceted obelisk; the two different Liberty crowns; Mellon’s pyramid and strong cornice; and Cira’s irregular glass facets. The neighborhood layer draws on Philadelphia’s brick, marble, brownstone, projecting bays, ironwork, and mansards.

Existing lighting systems: PECO’s crown remains the animated anchor. Cira Centre’s concealed colored LEDs are a real facade system; Comcast Technology Center’s illuminated blade is real architecture. Proposed effects: the timings, window occupancy changes, moving cloud reflections, gentle foliage motion, rain, and leaf settling described here are artistic animation proposals, not claims about live building activity or a connection to real lighting controls.

  • Foster + Partners — Comcast Technology Center: vertical steps, sky gardens, glass elevators, illuminated blade.
  • Robert A. M. Stern Architects — Comcast Center: faceted obelisk, silvery glass, transparent crown and corners.
  • One Liberty Place and Two Liberty Place: related but distinct Jahn towers.
  • KPF — BNY Mellon Center: tapered form, stone podium, cornice, and metal pyramid.
  • Brandywine Realty Trust — Three Logan Square: building gallery and neighborhood context.
  • Pelli Clarke & Partners — FMC Tower: curved, chamfered glass architecture at University City.
  • Enclos — Cira Centre: engineered faceted curtainwall and concealed colored LEDs.
  • Preservation Alliance — Rittenhouse West architectural walking tour: neighborhood building references.

Center City and University City occupy different sides of the Schuylkill. Broad panorama concepts intentionally compose these layers; they should not be mistaken for a verified view from a particular Rittenhouse address. The final direction can prioritize strict geography or a carefully composed Philadelphia setting.
